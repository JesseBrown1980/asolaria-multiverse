## Why four is a human artifact, in exact degrees

Two unit circles that *touch* while resting against a central one subtend exactly **60°**
at the centre. So the ring divides with no remainder:

```
360 / 60 = 6   exactly, remainder 0
```

Four seasons is the circle cut by two perpendicular axes — two solstices and two
equinoxes. It is a quantisation of something continuous, and it is not even the tight one:

```
four circles use     4 × 60° = 240° of 360°
left unused          120°   (333 per mille of the ring)
six circles use      6 × 60° = 360°, gap 0
```

Four does not fail because it is wrong about the sky. It fails because **it leaves a third
of the ring empty** and calls the emptiness a boundary.

## The sphere, verified by construction

Spherical seasons in time — so ask the same question off the plane. Twelve unit spheres
placed at the cuboctahedron's vertices, coordinates scaled by 2 so every number is an
integer, all permutations of `(±2, ±2, 0)`:

```
vertices                    12
squared norm, every one     8   (all identical)
min squared pair distance   8
touching pairs at that distance   24
pairs closer than that      0   ← overlaps
each touches the centre     yes — squared norm equals min pair distance
```

Twelve spheres touch the middle one and none of them overlap. That count is a proved
theorem — Schütte and van der Waerden settled it in 1953 after Newton and Gregory argued
about whether it was 12 or 13 — and the construction above is checked here in integers
rather than trusted.

**Twelve around a free centre is thirteen.** The centre is not one of the twelve; it is the
thing they are all touching, and it stays free.
