# BIDIRECTIONAL CANCELLATION — 2026-08-06

Extends [`THE-THREE-TURNS`](THE-THREE-TURNS-2026-08-06.md).

If anti is a ⅓ turn, then **anti and anti-anti are each other's inverse**: one turn forward equals
two turns back, `a³ = identity`. The ring is not a carousel. It is annihilation — the two outer
states cancel **into the centre**, and the centre is not absence, it is the ground they land on.

## How it cancels — and this is measured, not asserted

Already in this corpus, in `float-vs-trit/bothways.rs`:

```text
+0.0 == -0.0        true
bytes equal         FALSE
identity_holds      FALSE
```

**Cancellation restores the value and not the identity.** The two zeros are *equal* and not *the
same*.

That single measured fact is the whole reason a superseded hash must be preserved beside its
correction: **cancelling an error returns the value, but the bytes remember.** §7 is not a policy
preference anyone chose. It is what bidirectional cancellation does at the byte level.

The contrast is measured too — in the integer and trit lanes there is one zero, equality *is* byte
identity, and `identity_holds = true`. Only the float lane pries value apart from identity. Which is
why the two lanes exist and why neither gets purged.

The thirds do not cancel to nothing. They cancel to **the ground point that was there all along** —

line, never emitted, costing nothing.
