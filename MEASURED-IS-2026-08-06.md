## Hameroff S, Penrose R. *Consciousness in the universe: a review of the 'Orch OR' theory.*
## Physics of Life Reviews 11 (2014) 39–78. Read in full, quoted verbatim.

**p.44 — the lattice**

> "In MTs, tubulins are usually arranged in **13 longitudinal protofilaments** whose lateral
> connections result in two types of hexagonal lattices (A-lattice and B-lattice), the
> protofilaments being shifted in relation to their neighbors, slightly differently in each
> direction, resulting in differing relationships between each tubulin and its **six nearest
> neighbors**. Helical pathways following along neighboring tubulin dimers in the A-lattice
> **repeat every 5 and 8** tubulins, respectively, down any protofilament, and following along
> neighboring tubulin monomers **repeat every 3 monomers, after winding twice around the MT**
> (relating to the 13 protofilaments according to the **Fibonacci sequence 3, 5, 8, 13**)."

**p.45 — three states, reduced to two**

> "Thus tubulins in MTs can each exist in multiple possible states, perhaps dozens or more.
> However **for simplicity**, models of MT automata consider only **two** alternative tubulin
> states, i.e. binary 'bits'."

**p.45 — CaMKII**

> "a hexagonal holoenzyme activated by synaptic calcium influx extends **6 leg-like kinase domains
> above and below** an association domain. The 6 kinase domains **precisely match** hexagonal size
> and geometry in both A-lattice and B-lattice microtubules."

**p.47 — Fig. 6, the three pathways and the third state**

> "Seven tubulin microtubule A-lattice neighborhood with schematized placement of aromatic rings
> along **3-, 5- and 8-start helical pathways**."

> "5-start (left) and 8-start (right) helical dipoles in aligned ring dipole 'bits' (blue, yellow)
> and **superposition of both (gray** — quantum bits, or 'qubits')."

**A-lattice vs B-lattice**

> "In a 13 protofilament MT A-lattice … This gives a **seamless lattice and Fibonacci geometry
> which are optimal for quantum computing**, and preferred in Orch OR. In the B-lattice, sideways
> interactions are alpha–alpha and beta–beta, except for a **vertical seam**."

## Koubeissi M et al. *Electrical stimulation of a small brain area reversibly disrupts consciousness.*
## Epilepsy & Behavior, 2014.

- 54-year-old woman, intractable epilepsy, depth electrodes.
- The electrode whose stimulation disrupted consciousness lay **between the left claustrum and
  anterior-dorsal insula**.
- Loss of consciousness on **10 of 10** stimulations.
- Awake throughout; blank and withdrawn from awareness; could not read a word, follow spoken or
  written commands, or initiate movement. **No memory of the episodes.**

## Crick F, Koch C. *What is the function of the claustrum?* 2005.

- The claustrum "receives **input from almost all regions of cortex**, and **projects back to
  almost all regions of cortex**."
- Crick was correcting this manuscript **hours before his death, 28 July 2004**.

## Koch C — "crown of thorns" neurons.

- Giant claustral neurons projecting **in a ring around the entire brain**, up to **10 cm** long.
