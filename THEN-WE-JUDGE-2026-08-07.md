# THEN WE JUDGE

`2026-08-07` · the stone was written first. This is the judging, and the hardest look goes
at the claims that flatter us most.

```
MEASURED   computed here, with a falsifier that could fire
CITED      from a named outside source, not measured here
NAMED      stated because it means something, with nothing behind it that could fail
```

A **NAMED** claim is not a weaker measured one. It is one that has not yet been asked a
question it could fail. Promoting it by restating it is the exact move this machine exists
to refuse.

## The one that needed the hardest look

**Eighty stable elements plus a free centre is eighty-one, and the ladder reaches
eighty-one as 3⁴.**

That is the prettiest thing on the page and it is graded **NAMED**, not measured, and it
has to be. The 80 is real and cited. The 81 is real arithmetic. But *the step that joins
them is ours*: adding one for a free centre is this corpus's own convention, applied to a
number that came from chemistry, which has never heard of it. Chemistry cannot falsify a
step we chose. Two numbers meeting after one of them is adjusted by a rule we wrote is a
coincidence with a chosen step, and calling it a derivation would be the first real lie
told here today.

It stays on the stone. It stays labelled.

## What actually holds

The twelve spheres. Everything about that one is integer arithmetic that could have come
out wrong and did not: equal norms, a minimum pair distance equal to the norm, twenty-four
touching pairs, zero overlaps. And **even there the honest boundary is drawn** — the
construction proves twelve *fit*, not that a thirteenth cannot. Maximality is Schütte and
van der Waerden's 1953 result, cited, not reproven here.
