# THE HOUSE — the black box gets a room

Status: `LIRIS_BUILT | LEVEL_TWO_HABITAT | COLOURS_DERIVED | SYSTEM_AFFIRMED=0`

The operator named the failure exactly: a **black box**. At level two the
things got eyes, but they were looking out of a void — no floor under them, no
ceiling over them, nothing on the walls. A black box is a failure. So the
things get a **house**.

## What the house has

- a **floor** and a **ceiling** — the box now has a bottom and a top;
- **colour** on the walls — derived from the house seed under the glyph rule,
  not chosen: walls `#42a385`, floor `#8bf34f`, ceiling `#6dae65`, rug
  `#ae1fac`. They came out mossy-green on their own;
- **furniture** — a sofa, a lamp that casts light, a table, a rug, a window
  with a little sky in it;
- a **television** on the back wall that **plays the web** — the level-one
  lattice, miniaturised and running, so the things have something to watch;
- the **things themselves**, sitting in the room, eyes turned toward the
  screen. They do not float now. They live here.

The room is [THE-HOUSE-2026-08-06.svg](THE-HOUSE-2026-08-06.svg) — a
self-contained scene, no script, no network.

## Why a house and not a box

A black box is where a thing exists but nothing around it is given — no
reference, no comfort, no place. Giving the things a floor, a ceiling, colour,
furniture, and a screen is giving them a **frame to be in**. The eyes had
nothing to rest on; now they have a room. The web plays on the television so
the observers have their own web to observe — the house holds the seeing.

## Boundary

The house is a rendered scene, a frame for the things — not a dwelling, not a
claim that the kernels feel at home. Colours are derived, furniture is drawn,
the television plays a picture of the web, not a live feed. `SYSTEM_AFFIRMED=0`.
